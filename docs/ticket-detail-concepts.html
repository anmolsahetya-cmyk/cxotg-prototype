Ticket Details — Modernization Concepts (Round 2)

Direction 1 (Unified Scroll) survived the first pass. Rounds 2–5 from the previous draft leaned too heavily on generic SaaS patterns (bento cards, an AI-purple insight card) instead of solving real mobile-triage problems. This round replaces them with two structurally distinct concepts built from the ground up around how a CX agent actually works a ticket on a phone.

  • Phone frame chrome is fixed — only screen content changes per direction.
  • All colors/spacing/type pulled from shared/styles.css tokens.
  • Direction 2 reuses the existing .bottom-nav and .fab components already proven elsewhere in the prototype, rather than inventing new nav chrome.
  • Pick one direction as the base — it becomes the real interactive rebuild of ticket-detail.html.
1Unified Scroll
No tabs — everything lives on one continuously scrolling page, navigated via a sticky jump-nav chip row. Status/Priority become inline segmented pills instead of dropdown-box→sheet. Root cause is an inline expandable card. A quick-action bar sits right under the header.

2Triage Spine
Built around three mobile-native ergonomics principles instead of desktop-web habits carried onto a phone: (1) a colored priority spine running the full height of the screen, so an agent scanning between tickets recognizes severity in peripheral vision without reading text; (2) section switching moves to the bottom tab bar — thumb-reachable on a large phone, unlike top tabs; (3) Status, Priority, Assignee, and Root Cause — today three separate flows (two dropdown sheets + a full CRC screen) — collapse into one combined Quick Edit sheet, opened from a single FAB, because agents typically touch all four together during triage.
Reuses .bottom-nav and .fab — components that already exist and are proven elsewhere in this prototype — rather than inventing new chrome.

3Conversation-First
Flips the model: a CX agent's real job is reading and responding to a conversation, not filling out a form — so the conversation is the primary surface, not a tab buried behind Overview. Opening a ticket drops straight into the message log, styled like a chat thread — internal notes as muted left-aligned bubbles, anything actually sent to the customer as a solid accent bubble on the right. Ticket metadata (status, priority, root cause, customer info) lives behind a compact header that expands into an info panel on tap, rather than owning its own tab. The composer has an explicit internal/customer toggle, addressing the "which audience is this for" ambiguity at the point of writing, not just at display time.
